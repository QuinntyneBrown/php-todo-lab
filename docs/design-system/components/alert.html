Alert

A message in the layout about a condition that lasts: the list failed to load, or the browser is offline.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableUsed in v1

Overview

An alert describes a condition that holds until something changes: the list could not load, or the browser is offline. It sits in the layout, not over it, and stays until the condition clears. The product uses two placements: the banner inside the task card in place of the list (L2-008.5) and the notice above the card (L2-042.3).

  • Use an alert for state that persists. Use a toast for the result of a single action.
  • Use an inline message for a problem with one field.
  • An alert never blocks the rest of the page: the composer keeps working under both v1 alerts.

Anatomy

  1. Icon24px, --alert-icon (the tone's icon colour); aria-hidden. Tone is never colour alone (D19).
  2. BodyMedium-weight title or single sentence in --color-fg-default; optional regular-weight detail.
  3. Actions (optional)Outline buttons that take the alert's tone.
  4. Dismiss (optional)Ghost icon button, only for alerts the person may hide while the condition persists.
  5. ContainerTone background, hairline tone border, --radius-md, 16px padding.

Variants

Tones

Four tones; pick by consequence, not by mood. Danger: something failed and needs action. Warning: something will fail. Success: a lasting state is now good. Info: neutral context.

Responsive behaviour

  • The body has flex: 1 1 12.5rem; when the row is narrower than body + actions (XS), the actions wrap to their own line, left-aligned under the icon. From SM the Try again button sits at the end of the line.
  • The banner is flush with the card edges at every breakpoint; the notice keeps its 12px radius and 16px gap above the card.
  • Text never truncates; at 320px with 200% zoom the banner text wraps to four lines and the button stays reachable.

Theming

Each tone maps five component tokens: --alert-bg, --alert-border, --alert-icon, --alert-action and --alert-solid, all from the status roles (--color-danger-bg …). Body text stays --color-fg-default in every tone, as in the mocks. Buttons inside an alert become outline buttons in the tone: label --alert-action, border --alert-solid, hover fill --color-bg-surface. In dark the backgrounds become deep tints (--palette-red-900, --palette-sun-950) with light tone text.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Plain content with an APG Alert role only when it appears in response to something: the load-error banner uses role="alert" because it replaces the loading state; the offline notice uses role="status" because it is informative and not urgent. An alert present at page load and unchanged has no role.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabMoves to the alert's actions in DOM order; the alert itself is not focusable.
Enter, SpaceActivates Try again or Dismiss.
EscNo effect.

Focus

Focus stays where it was when an alert appears. After Try again succeeds, focus moves to the composer (the list replaced the button that had focus). After Dismiss, focus moves to the next focusable element after the alert.

Labelling

The sentence is the message; no aria-label. The icon is aria-hidden, so the text must carry the meaning on its own. Dismiss buttons are labelled "Dismiss".

Motion

Alerts do not animate in; they replace content in place. Only the buttons transition colour over --duration-fast.

Touch

Try again and Dismiss are 44px targets. The banner never relies on hover.

Content

  • Formula: what happened, then what to do. "Couldn't load your tasks. Check your connection and try again."
  • Name the consequence in warnings: "Changes will fail until you reconnect."
  • Contractions are fine ("Couldn't", "You're"); no apologies, no exclamation marks, no "Error:" prefixes (L2-025).
  • Action labels are verbs that match the outcome: "Try again", not "Retry" or "OK".
